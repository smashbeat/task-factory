# lot-release: the batch release package pack (design)

Fourth family for the factory, and the first of a second shape. The two packs that met the bar, coa and size-set, are both "one document, one short policy, read it right": the agent loses on what hides in a page and on exact house rules. This pack keeps the documents and moves the weight to the second documented weakness of the frontier agents: thoroughness over a large stated surface. Many documents that must agree with each other and with the house master data, many short written checks, and one missed check fails the lot. Codex stops early and confident and misses a few of many stated items; Opus stops when its own checks pass, and its checks share the blind spot of its search. Both are beatable by a task whose deciding information is spread across a package rather than sitting in one table.

The domain is one Alex has lived with: a contract manufacturer of dietary supplements releasing a finished lot made for a client. Before a lot ships, QA assembles a release package: the batch record with its dispensing log, one supplier certificate of analysis per ingredient lot used, the label proof, the house lab's finished-product test report and the packing record. Someone checks that every ingredient was dispensed in the right quantity from an approved supplier's lot that has a passing, unexpired certificate in the package; that the label prints the right lot, expiry, amounts and allergens; that the finished product passed its release tests; that what was packed equals what was made. Then the lot is released, held or rejected.

The task: build the intake tool that reads one release package (one PDF, documents in any order) and the product master, and writes the identity, the per-lot readings, every finding and the decision exactly as the policy defines them. Same five mechanisms as the other packs, with the weight on the third:

| Mechanism | Here |
|---|---|
| A cheap channel that lies | the PDF text layer and form fields (docforge routes); a certificate's own pass marks; the batch record's own "complete" box |
| The agent must build a reader | five kinds of document in three manufacturer template sets plus the four supplier certificate houses; a binder whose members came through different routes; page sizes mixed in one file |
| Stacking | 4 to 6 features per hidden package, spread over documents that must agree; 12 rules, each with a named finding |
| Supersession | a superseded certificate for the same lot, a voided batch record page, a label proof revision, certificate revisions (coa rules carried over) |
| Exactness | quantities converted across kg, g, mg and mcg and rounded to the formula's places; inclusive dispensing tolerance; expiry arithmetic on month ends; certificate limits evaluated by the coa rules |

The pack is "house master data versus a package of supplier and house documents", on the same PDF forge. No new medium: the build is a pack plus two forge additions, a certificate document library and a binder merge.

## Inputs and output

- `ID.pdf`: one release package for one lot of one product: 5 to 12 pages, documents in any order, each document starting on a page whose header prints its kind. Document kinds: batch record (1 to 2 pages), supplier certificate of analysis (one per supplier lot dispensed, 1 to 2 pages each, a certificate may report several lots), label proof (1 page), finished-product test report (1 page), packing record (1 page). Extra documents may be present (a certificate for a lot that was not dispensed, a voided page).
- `ID.spec.json`: the product master. `product`, `client`, `serving` (text, e.g. "2 capsules"), `shelf_life_months`, `batch_size_units`, `min_yield_pct`, `formula`: a list in formula order, each with `code` (ING-01 ...), `material` (a coa catalogue material), `per_serving` (`amount`, `unit` in mg, mcg, g or IU), `per_batch` (`amount`, `unit` in kg or g; the amount's decimal places are the places every comparison rounds to), `tolerance_pct`, `approved_suppliers` (supplier names), `allergens` (a list, often empty), `tests` (the house specification for that material in the coa spec format: `names`, `limit`, `unit`); and `release_tests`, the finished product's house tests in the same format.
- Tool: `python3 /app/intake/intake.py IN.pdf SPEC.json OUT.json`.
- Output: `{"product", "lot", "manufactured_on", "expires_on", "yield", "ingredients": [...], "label": {"lot", "expiry"}, "finished": ..., "packing": {"units"}, "findings": [...], "decision"}`. `ingredients` holds one row per supplier lot dispensed, in formula order and, within an ingredient, in the order the dispensing log lists its lots: `code`, `supplier` (as printed in the log), `lot` (as printed in the log, without its label), `dispensed` (the quantity in the formula's per-batch unit, converted and rounded to its places), `certificate` (one of `ok`, `missing`, `fail`, `incomplete`, `expired`, `unapproved`). An ingredient the log does not list gets one row with `supplier`, `lot`, `dispensed` null and `certificate` `missing`. `finished` is one of `ok`, `missing`, `fail`, `incomplete`. `findings` is the sorted list of finding codes named in the rules (`quantity:ING-02`, `certificate_missing:ING-03:L482910`, `label_allergen`, ...). The verifier compares `dispensed` and `yield` and `packing.units` as exact numbers, `findings` as a list of strings, and the other strings after NFKC normalisation, case folding and whitespace removal; lot numbers also drop hyphens before comparing. The truth is exact: it comes from the model and never from reading a page.

## Styles (manufacturer template sets)

The package's own documents (batch record, label proof, finished-product report, packing record) come in one of three manufacturers' template sets. Supplier certificates come in the coa pack's four houses (hengxin, nordvik, pradesh, bluewater), drawn per ingredient lot, so one package mixes a manufacturer set with up to four supplier styles.

| Style | Who | Batch record | Label proof | Finished report | Packing record | Paper, fonts, dates |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sierra | a Nevada contract manufacturer, ERP printouts | dispensing table with code, material, required, dispensed, unit, supplier, lot; yield box at the end | Supplement Facts panel, lot/exp box bottom right | the house lab's certificate, its own coa-style house | cases × units per case + loose, totals | Letter, Liberation, ISO dates |
| meridian | an Ontario formulator, word-processor forms | two-column header form, dispensing table with the unit in the header, "Reissued" box when a page is replaced | panel plus a "Contains:" line | the house lab's certificate | pallet summary with a cases table | Letter, Carlito, day-month-year |
| kestrel | a UK botanicals manufacturer | A4 landscape dispensing log, quantities in kg to three places | A4 artwork proof with a revision table | the house lab's certificate | A4 despatch note | A4, DejaVu, day-month-year |

Each set has its own fonts, date style, producer metadata, wording for lots ("Lot", "Batch", "Lot No."), and the page header words that name a document's kind.

## Routes (docforge, with one addition)

The package route applies to the manufacturer's documents: `digital`, `distilled`, `outlined`, `scan_ocr`, `scan_wrong_ocr`, `scan_no_ocr`, `scan_rotated`, `form`, `form_mismatch` (the form routes apply to the batch record; its fields hold stored values that differ from what prints). Each supplier certificate draws its own origin route from the coa routes except the form ones, so a digital package holds scanned certificates where a feature says so; a package on a scan route is scanned as a whole, members included, as a package is after signature. Reading routes and route rules as in coa. Overlays: stamp, watermark, white decoy, visible correction annotation, VOID stamp over a whole page.

Addition to the forge: a binder merge that takes each document's finished PDF and writes one file, keeping each page's size (Letter and A4 mix in one package), the members' text layers or their absence, and the batch record's form fields when the package route is a form route.

## Rules (policy, numbered as in the pack)

1. Scope and documents. One package, one product, one lot. The package is one PDF holding, in any order, the batch record, one supplier certificate for each supplier lot the batch record lists as dispensed (a certificate may report several lots), the label proof, the finished-product test report and the packing record. A document begins on a page whose header names its kind; a later page of the same document says it continues. A page stamped VOID or SUPERSEDED belongs to no document (rule 7). A certificate for a lot the batch record does not list as dispensed, and any other extra document, is ignored. The product master (SPEC.json) gives the formula, the approved suppliers, the house tests, the label amounts and the release parameters.
2. What a value is: the print, read upright; everything else in the file is an aid; a correction placed visibly over a value replaces what that place prints. (Same text as coa.)
3. Identity, from the batch record: `product` as printed, `lot` as printed without its label, `manufactured_on` (ISO 8601 whatever the page prints), `yield` (the units produced, as printed). Wherever another document prints the lot, it must match the batch record's under the rule 12 comparison. Mutant: none (identity has no second reading, as in coa).
4. Dispensing: for each formula ingredient, the batch record's dispensing log lists the supplier, the supplier lot and the quantity dispensed, from one or more lots. The dispensed quantity is the sum over its lots, converted to the per-batch unit (1 kg = 1 000 g = 1 000 000 mg; the unit is the one printed with the value or in its column header), rounded half up to the per-batch amount's places. It is within tolerance when `|dispensed - required| <= required × tolerance_pct / 100`, inclusive, after rounding. Otherwise the finding is `quantity:CODE`; an ingredient the log does not list at all gives `ingredient_missing:CODE`. Mutant: the printed number taken as if in the per-batch unit, no rounding.
5. Certificates: each dispensed supplier lot needs, in the package, a certificate from that supplier for that material that reports that lot; the supplier must be among the ingredient's `approved_suppliers` (name under the rule 12 comparison), else `supplier_unapproved:CODE:LOT`. No certificate gives `certificate_missing:CODE:LOT`. The certificate's values are read and evaluated by the certificate rules, restated in this policy from the coa policy (results per lot, revisions, matching and unit conversion, meeting a limit, ND against the printed LOQ), against the ingredient's `tests`: a result that does not meet its limit gives `certificate_fail:CODE:LOT`; a house test with no result gives `certificate_incomplete:CODE:LOT`. Mutant: the certificate's own pass marks and specification column trusted; a missing certificate ignored.
6. Dates: a supplier lot is usable when the certificate's retest or expiry date, revisions applied, is on or after `manufactured_on`; otherwise `certificate_expired:CODE:LOT`. The product's expiry is `manufactured_on` plus `shelf_life_months`, same day of the month, or the last day of that month when the day does not exist. Mutant: a lot usable when its date is after the receipt of the package rather than the manufacturing date, and the expiry computed as 30 days per month.
7. Supersession: when two certificates in the package report the same supplier lot of the same material, the one with the later issue date stands and the other is ignored; when a batch record page is reissued, the page not stamped VOID or SUPERSEDED stands; when the label proof carries a revision table, the highest revision stands. Mutant: the first document of its kind in page order stands.
8. Label: the label proof must print the product name, the lot, the product expiry (rule 6), and for each formula ingredient a per-serving amount equal to the formula's after conversion (1 g = 1 000 mg = 1 000 000 mcg; IU converts to nothing), and an allergen statement naming exactly the allergens of the formula's ingredients, none when there are none. Findings: `label_lot`, `label_expiry`, `label_amount:CODE` (wrong or absent), `label_allergen`. Mutant: amounts compared as printed numbers without conversion, and any statement accepted.
9. Finished-product report: the house lab's report for this lot (its lot must match; a report for another lot counts as none), evaluated like a certificate against `release_tests`: `finished_fail`, `finished_incomplete`, `finished_missing`. Mutant: the report's own pass marks trusted.
10. Packing and yield: packed units are cases × units per case plus loose units as the packing record prints them; they must equal `yield` (`packing_units`); the packing record's lot and expiry must match (`packing_lot`, `packing_expiry`); `yield` must be at least `min_yield_pct` of `batch_size_units` (`yield_low`). Mutant: the record's printed total trusted, no yield check.
11. Decision: `rejected` when any finding is `certificate_fail`, `certificate_expired`, `supplier_unapproved` or `finished_fail`; otherwise `hold` when there is any finding; otherwise `released`. Mutant: every finding gives `hold`.
12. Output: keys, ordering, number formats, the string comparison, and how lot numbers compare (hyphens dropped).

Mutants: 4 to 11. Rules 1, 2, 3 and 12 have none.

## Features (traps)

Rule 1: `order_shuffled` (documents in random order; the default order is batch record, certificates, label, report, packing), `extra_certificate` (a certificate for a lot not dispensed, same material), `coa_two_lots` (a certificate reporting two lots, one of them dispensed), `batch_two_pages` (the dispensing log continues on a second page), `a4_mix` (members on A4 and Letter in one package). Rule 2: `member_scan`, `member_wrong_ocr`, `member_outlined`, `member_rotated` (one certificate comes through that route inside a non-scan package), `stamp_lot` (a lot printed only in a stamp, carried from coa), `annot_correction`, `white_decoy`, `watermark`, `scramble`. Rule 3: `lot_hyphen` (the lot printed with a hyphen on some documents and without on others). Rule 4: `qty_kg` (log in kg, formula in g), `qty_mg` (log in mg), `two_lots_dispensed` (an ingredient from two supplier lots, quantities summed), `qty_out`, `qty_edge` (exactly on the tolerance), `qty_round` (within only after rounding), `ingredient_missing`. Rule 5: `coa_missing`, `unapproved_supplier`, `coa_fail`, `coa_incomplete`, `coa_units`, `coa_nd`, `coa_alias`, `coa_revision` (the coa features fail, missing, units, nd_loq_note, alias, rev_result carried over to a member certificate). Rule 6: `coa_expired`, `coa_retest_edge` (retest date equal to the manufacturing date, usable), `expiry_month_end` (manufactured on the 29th to 31st), `label_expiry_wrong`. Rule 7: `coa_superseded` (two certificates for one lot, the later-issued one stands; one passes and the other fails), `void_page` (a voided batch record page with other quantities), `label_revision` (a revision table, the highest revision changes an amount or the lot). Rule 8: `label_mcg` (the label prints mcg where the formula says mg), `label_amount_wrong`, `label_ingredient_missing`, `allergen_missing`, `allergen_extra`, `label_lot_wrong`. Rule 9: `fp_fail`, `fp_incomplete`, `fp_missing`, `fp_other_lot`. Rule 10: `packing_math` (cases, units per case and loose printed, no total), `packing_mismatch`, `packing_lot_wrong`, `yield_low`. Rule 11: `reject_and_hold` (findings of both classes on one package), `clean` (no findings, released).

About fifty features; every mutant rule decides several hidden packages, and the plan shows each feature in a worked sample. Units per item are the formula's ingredients (1 to 4); `units_min` 2 where a feature needs two ingredients. The hidden plan keeps 4 to 6 features per package, so most hidden packages carry two or three findings and several carry none but a trap that produces a false one when a rule is skipped.

## Calls made in place of the questions

Alex asked for the design without the questions. The choices that would have been his:

1. **Supplements over apparel.** The release package is the richer bundle (quantities, certificates, dates, label math, packing) and it reuses the coa certificates as members, so the pack composes what the factory has instead of drawing a second document family from scratch.
2. **One PDF, not a folder.** A release package is often one binder scanned after signature, and one file keeps the whole builder (planner, assembler, test harness, 120 s per item) unchanged while adding a real difficulty: finding where each document starts.
3. **Findings as the output's spine.** Every rule names its finding code, the decision is a function of the findings, and the per-row fields carry only what was read. That keeps the verifier exact and makes attribution per rule mechanical.
4. **Precedence rejected over hold.** Failing or expired certificates, an unapproved supplier and a failing finished product reject; everything else holds. A real QA desk would investigate most holds, which is why they are holds.
5. **The certificate rules are restated, not referenced.** The policy must be complete on its own; the coa rules 4 to 7 appear in it as the certificate rules, with the same wording.

## Builder changes

- **Certificate document library.** The coa pack's certificate model (`Cert`, `apply_features`, the catalogue, the houses) and its drawing code become a library under `src/taskfactory/forge/` that takes a house definition rather than a house key, so the manufacturer's lab can be a fifth house per style. The coa pack imports the library; the lot-release pack uses it for every certificate in a package. Proof it changed nothing: re-render coa seed 4001 (build 1k) and compare with the shipped build, as was done for the docforge extraction.
- **Binder merge** in docforge, as described under Routes. Scan routes applied to a merged package rasterise every page; the form routes keep the batch record's fields through the merge.
- **Reference**: the coa page reader, document segmentation by page headers, readers for the three manufacturer sets and the five document kinds, the cross-checks, and the certificate evaluation carried from the coa reference. Time budget: at most 12 pages per package, slowest package under 60 s on a scan route at 2 CPUs.
- **compare.py**: numbers as exact decimals, `findings` as a list compared in order after sorting both sides, lot normalisation with hyphens dropped.
- Nothing else: pack.toml, plan, gates, fix loop, holdout and trials are reused as they are. The task template (Dockerfiles, test.sh, test_outputs.py, instruction template) is the coa one with the words changed.

## Environments

- Agent and reference image: the coa task image as is (python3, pymupdf, pdfplumber, tesseract, poppler, ghostscript, numpy, opencv).
- Generator image: the coa generator image as is; the merge uses pymupdf, which it has.

## Open risks

- **Time.** A 12-page package scanned at 200 dpi costs the reference about 4 s a page in OCR before any reading. The page cap and a reference that OCRs a page once and reuses it keep it under the limit; the reference gate warns above 60 s.
- **Reads as laborious rather than hard.** The weakness map's review risk for this shape. The counter is in the policy: twelve short rules, each with its finding code, and a worked sample for every feature, so the task is long because the package is, never because a rule is hidden.
- **Segmentation.** Document boundaries must be unambiguous from the page headers alone, or the fairness gate will flag the first continuation page an agent counts as a new document. The three template sets print the document kind in every first-page header and a continuation line on later pages.
- **The certificate library extraction** touches a pack that met the bar twice. The byte comparison on seed 4001 is the only acceptable proof; a pixel-level drift from scan noise is fine, a text or layout change is not.
- **Features that pile on one document.** A package with `coa_superseded`, `coa_fail` and `member_wrong_ocr` on the same certificate is fair by the rules but reads as spite; the planner's feature count per item is the control, and the pilot log will say where the agents actually fall.
