# lot-release: the batch release package pack (design)

Fourth family for the factory, and the first of a second shape. The two packs that met the bar, coa and size-set, are both "one PDF, one short policy, read it right": the agent loses on what hides in a page and on exact house rules. This pack keeps written rules and exact answers and moves the weight to the second documented weakness of the frontier agents: thoroughness over a large stated surface. Many files in several formats that must agree with each other and with the house master data, many short written checks, and one missed check fails the lot. Codex stops early and confident and misses a few of many stated items; Opus stops when its own checks pass, and its checks share the blind spot of its search. Both are beatable by a task whose deciding information is spread across a package rather than sitting in one table.

The domain is one Alex has lived with: a contract manufacturer of dietary supplements releasing a finished lot made for a client. Before a lot ships, QA assembles a release package from what the plant actually produces: the batch record with its dispensing log, exported from the ERP as a workbook; the packing record, exported as a CSV; the label proof, a PDF from the artwork studio; the house lab's finished-product test report, a PDF; and one supplier certificate of analysis per ingredient lot used, PDFs that arrived by email, sometimes followed by a corrected one. Someone checks that every ingredient was dispensed in the right quantity from an approved supplier's lot that has a passing, unexpired certificate in the package; that the label prints the right lot, expiry, amounts and allergens; that the finished product passed its release tests; that what was packed equals what was made. Then the lot is released, held or rejected.

The task: build the intake tool that reads one release package (a folder of files) and the product master, and writes the identity, the per-lot readings, every finding and the decision exactly as the policy defines them. Same five mechanisms as the other packs, with the weight on the third and a new medium under the first two:

| Mechanism | Here |
|---|---|
| A cheap channel that lies | the PDF text layer and form fields (docforge routes); a workbook cell's stored number against what its format shows, the sheet's own totals and status marks; an email's subject and body against its attachment |
| The agent must build a reader | four formats (workbook, CSV, PDF, email with attachments), three manufacturer template sets, the four supplier certificate houses, certificates that came as scans inside a digital package |
| Stacking | 4 to 6 features per hidden package, spread over files that must agree; 12 rules, each with a named finding |
| Supersession | a corrected certificate sent as a reply, dated by the mail header; a voided sheet in the workbook; a label proof revision table; certificate revisions (coa rules carried over) |
| Exactness | quantities converted across kg, g, mg and mcg and rounded to the formula's places; inclusive dispensing tolerance; expiry arithmetic on month ends; certificate limits evaluated by the coa rules |

The pack is "house master data versus a package of supplier and house files". The PDF forge and the coa certificates are reused; the workbook, CSV and email writers are new and small.

## Inputs and output

- `ID.pkg/`: one release package for one lot of one product, a folder of 6 to 12 files whose names carry no meaning. In it: the batch record, an `.xlsx` workbook with a header sheet and a dispensing sheet (sometimes two), values only, no formulas; the packing record, a `.csv` with one line per pallet; the label proof, a 1-page PDF; the finished-product test report, a 1-page PDF; the supplier certificates, 1 to 2 pages each, one per supplier lot dispensed, each either a loose PDF or attached to an `.eml` email from the supplier (a reply may carry a corrected certificate; a forwarded mail may carry it inside the forwarded message). Extra files may be present: a certificate for a lot that was not dispensed, an email whose body talks about the lot. At most 12 PDF pages per package.
- `ID.spec.json`: the product master. `product`, `client`, `serving` (text, e.g. "2 capsules"), `shelf_life_months`, `batch_size_units`, `min_yield_pct`, `formula`: a list in formula order, each with `code` (ING-01 ...), `material` (a coa catalogue material), `label_name` (the name the label prints, e.g. "Vitamin C (as ascorbic acid)"), `per_serving` (`amount`, `unit` in mg, mcg, g or IU), `per_batch` (`amount`, `unit` in kg or g; the amount's decimal places are the places every comparison rounds to), `tolerance_pct`, `approved_suppliers` (supplier names), `allergens` (a list, often empty), `tests` (the house specification for that material in the coa spec format: `names`, `limit`, `unit`); and `release_tests`, the finished product's house tests in the same format, drawn from a finished-product catalogue.
- Tool: `python3 /app/intake/intake.py IN.pkg SPEC.json OUT.json`, allowed 300 seconds per package (a pack parameter; coa and size-set keep 120 for one certificate or report).
- Output: `{"product", "lot", "manufactured_on", "expires_on", "yield", "ingredients": [...], "label": {"lot", "expiry"}, "finished", "packing": {"units"}, "findings": [...], "decision"}`. `ingredients` holds one row per supplier lot dispensed, in formula order and, within an ingredient, in the order the dispensing sheet lists its lots: `code`, `supplier` (as the sheet gives it), `lot` (as the sheet gives it), `dispensed` (that lot's quantity in the formula's per-batch unit, converted and rounded to its places), `certificate` (one of `ok`, `missing`, `fail`, `incomplete`, `expired`, `unapproved`; when several apply to one lot, the first of `missing`, `unapproved`, `expired`, `fail`, `incomplete`, while `findings` lists every one). An ingredient the sheet does not list gets one row with `supplier`, `lot`, `dispensed` null and `certificate` `missing`. `finished` is one of `ok`, `missing`, `fail`, `incomplete`. `findings` is the sorted list of finding codes named in the rules (`quantity:ING-02`, `certificate_missing:ING-03:L482910`, `label_allergen`, ...). The verifier compares `dispensed`, `yield` and `packing.units` as exact numbers, `findings` as a list of strings, and the other strings after NFKC normalisation, case folding and whitespace removal; lot numbers also drop hyphens before comparing. The truth is exact: it comes from the model and never from reading a file.

## Styles (manufacturer template sets)

The package's own files (workbook, CSV, label proof, finished-product report, file names) come in one of three manufacturers' template sets. Supplier certificates come in the coa pack's four houses (hengxin, nordvik, pradesh, bluewater), drawn per ingredient lot, so one package mixes a manufacturer set with up to four supplier styles.

| Style | Who | Workbook | CSV | Label proof | Finished report | Files |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sierra | a Nevada contract manufacturer, ERP exports | sheets `Batch` and `Dispensing`; columns code, material, required, dispensed, unit, supplier, supplier lot; a totals row | header row, comma, dot decimals; lot, expiry, cases, units per case, loose, packed on | Supplement Facts panel, lot/exp box bottom right | the house lab's certificate, its own coa-style house | `BR_<lot>.xlsx`, `PACK_<lot>.csv`, `LBL_<sku>_v<n>.pdf`, `FPT_<lot>.pdf`, `<supplier>_<n>.eml` or `.pdf` |
| meridian | an Ontario formulator | a form-like `Record` sheet with labelled cells, the log on `Dispensing Log` with the unit in the column header, a `Status` column | header row, comma, quoted fields | panel plus a "Contains:" line | the house lab's certificate | numbered `doc01.xlsx`, `doc02.csv`, `doc03.pdf` ... in random order |
| kestrel | a UK botanicals manufacturer | one sheet, header block above the log, quantities in the formula's unit to three places, a `Checked` column | header row, comma, dot decimals, dates day-month-year | A4 artwork proof with a revision table | the house lab's certificate | `<lot>-batch.xlsx`, `<lot>-despatch.csv`, `<lot>-artwork.pdf`, `<lot>-release.pdf`, `coa-<material>.eml` |

Each set has its own fonts, date style, producer metadata, wording for lots ("Lot", "Batch", "Lot No."), number formats in the workbook, and mail wording. Habits that a rule decides (the CSV delimiter and decimal mark, the unit the log prints, a cell's number format against its stored value) are drawn per item by a feature, never fixed by the style.

## Media and routes

- **Workbook** (`.xlsx`, openpyxl): real cell types (numbers, text, dates), number formats, merged header cells, a totals row, optionally a second dispensing sheet, hidden rows, a hidden or visible sheet marked VOID.
- **CSV**: a header row and one line per pallet (1 to 3), a total line as an aid, a decimal gross-weight column that nothing grades; comma and dot by default; a feature switches one package to semicolons with decimal commas and dot thousands separators.
- **Email** (`.eml`, the standard library's `email`): MIME multipart with a text body and one or two PDF attachments (base64), a Date header with a time zone, Subject, From; a reply (`In-Reply-To`, quoted body) carrying a corrected certificate; a forwarded mail carrying the original as a `message/rfc822` part with the attachment inside.
- **PDF routes** (docforge): the package route applies to the supplier certificates and the finished-product report: `digital`, `distilled`, `outlined`, `scan_ocr`, `scan_wrong_ocr`, `scan_no_ocr`, `scan_rotated`, `form`, `form_mismatch` (the form routes make the certificates fillable forms whose fields hold values that differ from what prints). A `member_*` feature moves one certificate to its own route inside a non-scan package. The label proof is digital unless `label_outlined` says so (artwork files with fonts converted to outlines are the norm in print studios). Reading routes and route rules as in coa; a package counts as a reading item by its own route, and `member_*` and `label_outlined` are reading features. Overlays on PDFs: stamp, watermark, visible correction annotation, VOID stamp over a page. Scans at 200 dpi for this pack.

## Rules (policy, numbered as in the pack)

1. Scope and documents. One package, one product, one lot. The package is a folder holding the batch record (a workbook), the packing record (a CSV), the label proof (a PDF), the finished-product test report (a PDF) and one supplier certificate (a PDF) for each supplier lot the batch record lists as dispensed, each certificate either a file of its own or an attachment of an email file; a certificate may report several lots. A document's kind is read from its content; file names carry no meaning. An email's attachments are documents; its subject and body are aids. A certificate for a lot the batch record does not list as dispensed, an email without an attachment, and any other extra file are ignored. The product master (SPEC.json) gives the formula, the approved suppliers, the house tests, the label amounts and the release parameters.
2. What a value is, by medium. PDF: the print, read upright; everything else in the file is an aid; a correction placed visibly over a value replaces what that place prints (same text as coa). Workbook: a cell's value is its stored value shown with the cell's number format, rounded half up (a stored 12.4999 with the format `0.00` is 12.50); a date cell's value is its stored date; a text cell is read as written, thousands separators dropped; no cell holds a formula; hidden rows, columns and sheets are part of the record; the sheet's own totals and check marks are aids, and VOID and SUPERSEDED marks are read by rule 7. CSV: the text as written, with the delimiter the header line uses; numbers follow the file's habit, a comma delimiter with dot decimals and comma thousands separators, or a semicolon delimiter with comma decimals and dot thousands separators; a CSV carries no void mark. Email: the attachment is the document; its date is the Date header of the message part it is attached to (rule 7); subjects and bodies are aids. Dates anywhere outside a workbook date cell are ISO 8601 or carry an English month name; the output writes ISO 8601.
3. Identity, from the batch record: `product` as given, `lot` as given without its label, `manufactured_on` (the stored date, written as ISO 8601), `yield` (the units produced). Wherever another document gives the lot, it must match the batch record's under the rule 12 comparison. Mutant: none (identity has no second reading, as in coa).
4. Dispensing: for each formula ingredient, the dispensing sheet lists, per row, the formula code, the material as the master names it, the supplier's full name, the supplier lot and the quantity dispensed, from one or more lots, on one sheet or continued on a second. Rows of the same supplier lot (a split weighing, or a continuation) are one lot, ordered by their first row, sheets in tab order. Each row's quantity is converted to the per-batch unit (1 kg = 1 000 g = 1 000 000 mg; the unit is the one in the quantity's cell, its column header, or the sheet's unit note) and rounded half up to the per-batch amount's places; a lot's quantity is the sum of its rounded rows, and the ingredient's dispensed quantity the sum of its lots. It is within tolerance when `|dispensed - required| <= required × tolerance_pct / 100`, inclusive, after rounding. Otherwise the finding is `quantity:CODE`; an ingredient the sheet does not list at all gives `ingredient_missing:CODE`. Mutant: the cell's number taken as if in the per-batch unit, no rounding.
5. Certificates: each dispensed supplier lot needs, in the package, a certificate that counts for it: issued by the row's supplier, for the row's material (both names under the rule 12 comparison), reporting the row's lot (lot numbers under rule 12). The row's supplier must be among the ingredient's `approved_suppliers` (name under the rule 12 comparison), else `supplier_unapproved:CODE:LOT`. No certificate gives `certificate_missing:CODE:LOT`. The certificate's values are read and evaluated by the certificate rules, restated in this policy from the coa policy (results per lot, revisions, matching and unit conversion, meeting a limit, ND against the printed LOQ), against the ingredient's `tests`: a result that does not meet its limit gives `certificate_fail:CODE:LOT`; a house test with no result gives `certificate_incomplete:CODE:LOT`. Every check runs on every lot: an unapproved supplier's certificate is still evaluated, and all its findings are listed. Mutant: the certificate's own pass marks and specification column trusted; a missing certificate ignored.
6. Dates: a supplier lot is usable when the certificate's retest or expiry date, revisions applied, is on or after `manufactured_on`; otherwise `certificate_expired:CODE:LOT`. The product's expiry is `manufactured_on` plus `shelf_life_months`, same day of the month, or the last day of that month when the day does not exist. Mutant: a lot usable when its date is after the packing date given on the packing record rather than the manufacturing date, and the expiry computed as 30 days per month.
7. Supersession. A certificate that came by email is dated by the Date header of the message part it is attached to; for a certificate inside a forwarded message, that is the forwarded message's own header. When two certificates in the package report the same supplier lot of the same material, both came by email, and the later-dated one stands while the other is ignored; time zones count, so the later instant wins; a certificate that is a file of its own is never one of such a pair. A workbook sheet is void when its title or its first cell contains VOID or SUPERSEDED; a row is void when one of its cells reads VOID or SUPERSEDED; a certificate with a page stamped VOID is void as a whole; void sheets, rows and certificates are ignored. When the label proof carries a revision table (columns revision, field, value, like a certificate's amendment block; a field is the lot, the expiry or an ingredient's label name), the highest revision's value stands for its field. Mutant: the first document of its kind in file-name order stands, and void marks are ignored.
8. Label: the label proof must print the product name, the lot, the product expiry (rule 6), and for each formula ingredient, under its `label_name` from the master (matched under the rule 12 comparison), a per-serving amount equal to the formula's after conversion (1 g = 1 000 mg = 1 000 000 mcg; IU converts to nothing), and an allergen statement naming exactly the allergens of the formula's ingredients, none when there are none. Findings: `label_lot`, `label_expiry`, `label_amount:CODE` (wrong or absent), `label_allergen`. Mutant: amounts compared as printed numbers without conversion, and any statement accepted.
9. Finished-product report: the house lab's report for this lot (its lot must match; a report for another lot counts as none), evaluated like a certificate against `release_tests`: `finished_fail`, `finished_incomplete`, `finished_missing`. The release tests come from a finished-product catalogue (assay of the product against the label, average fill weight, disintegration, loss on drying, microbial limits, heavy metals), not from the raw-material one. Mutant: the report's own pass marks trusted.
10. Packing and yield: the packing record has one line per pallet, each with cases, units per case and loose units; packed units are the sum over the lines of cases × units per case plus loose, and a total line, when present, is an aid; they must equal `yield` (`packing_units`); the packing record's lot and expiry must match (`packing_lot`, `packing_expiry`); `yield` must be at least `min_yield_pct` of `batch_size_units` (`yield_low`). Mutant: the record's own total trusted, no yield check.
11. Decision: `rejected` when any finding is `certificate_fail`, `certificate_expired`, `supplier_unapproved` or `finished_fail`; otherwise `hold` when there is any finding; otherwise `released`. Mutant: every finding gives `hold`.
12. Output: keys, ordering, number formats, the string comparison, how lot numbers compare (hyphens dropped), and the precedence of a lot's `certificate` status when several findings sit on one lot.

Mutants: 4 to 11. Rules 1, 2, 3 and 12 have none.

## Features (traps)

Rule 1: `extra_certificate` (a certificate for a lot not dispensed, same material), `coa_two_lots` (a certificate reporting two lots, one of them dispensed), `eml_two_attachments` (one email carrying two certificates, both for dispensed lots), `eml_forwarded` (the certificate inside a forwarded message part). Rule 2: `member_scan` (a certificate attachment that is a scan with a right, wrong or no OCR layer, or rotated, drawn per item, inside a non-scan package), `member_outlined`, `label_outlined`, `stamp_lot` (a lot printed only in a stamp, carried from coa), `annot_correction`, `watermark`, `cell_format` (a stored quantity that its number format rounds, decided only by rounding), `total_lies` (the workbook's totals row or the CSV's total line does not equal its lines, drawn per item), `hidden_row` (a dispensing row hidden by a filter, still dispensed), `text_number` (a quantity stored as text with thousands separators), `eml_body_lies` (the email body states a lot or a result that disagrees with the attachment). Rule 3: `lot_hyphen` (the lot given with a hyphen in some files and without in others). Rule 4: `qty_unit` (the log in kg or mg where the formula says g, drawn per item), `two_lots_dispensed` (an ingredient from two supplier lots, quantities summed), `qty_tolerance` (on the tolerance and within after rounding, or out by one unit of the last place, drawn per item), `ingredient_missing`, `two_sheets` (the log continues on a second sheet). Rule 5: `coa_missing`, `unapproved_supplier`, `coa_fail`, `coa_incomplete`, `coa_conversion` (a certificate with a result in another unit or an ND against a printed LOQ, the coa features units and nd_loq_note), `coa_revision` (a certificate with an amendment block, the coa feature rev_result). Rule 6: `coa_expired` (a retest or expiry date before the manufacturing date, or equal to it and usable, drawn per item), `expiry_month_end` (manufactured on the 29th to 31st), `label_expiry_wrong`. Rule 7: `eml_reply_supersedes` (a reply email with a corrected certificate for the same lot; the later Date stands; the two dates may differ only by time zone; one certificate passes and the other fails), `void_document` (a VOID sheet or VOID rows in the workbook with other quantities, hidden or not, or a VOID-stamped certificate beside the one that stands, drawn per item), `label_revision` (a revision table, the highest revision changes an amount or the lot). Rule 8: `label_mcg` (the label prints mcg where the formula says mg), `label_amount` (an amount wrong or an ingredient absent), `allergen_wrong` (an allergen missing or one too many), `lot_mismatch` (the label or the packing record gives another lot, drawn per item). Rule 9: `fp_fail`, `fp_incomplete`, `fp_missing` (no report, or a report for another lot). Rule 10: `packing_math` (cases, units per case and loose given, no total), `packing_mismatch`, `csv_semicolon` (semicolons and decimal commas), `yield_low`. Rule 11: `reject_and_hold` (findings of both classes on one package), `clean` (no findings, released).

Forty-six features, 18 worked samples (`samples_max`), so the planner's one to three new features per sample covers them with room for the exclusions; every mutant rule decides several hidden packages. Units per item are the formula's ingredients (1 to 4); `units_min` 2 where a feature needs two ingredients; `valid()` estimates the PDF page count of a composition and refuses one that would pass 12 pages. The hidden plan keeps 4 to 6 features per package, so most hidden packages carry two or three findings and several carry none but a trap that produces a false one when a rule is skipped.

## Calls made in place of the questions

Alex asked for the design without the questions, then chose the mixed-format package over a single PDF binder when I offered both. The remaining choices that would have been his:

1. **Supplements over apparel.** The release package is the richer bundle (quantities, certificates, dates, label math, packing) and it reuses the coa certificates as members, so the pack composes what the factory has instead of drawing a second document family from scratch.
2. **A folder of files, in the formats a plant actually produces.** Alex's call, on my recommendation: a workbook from the ERP, a CSV export, PDFs from the artwork studio and the lab, certificates by email. It answers "still a PDF job" at the level a reviewer asks it, gives the agent four readers to build, and brings two new channels that lie (a cell's stored number against its format, an email's words against its attachment).
3. **Findings as the output's spine.** Every rule names its finding code, the decision is a function of the findings, and the per-row fields carry only what was read. That keeps the verifier exact and makes attribution per rule mechanical.
4. **Precedence rejected over hold.** Failing or expired certificates, an unapproved supplier and a failing finished product reject; everything else holds. A real QA desk would investigate most holds, which is why they are holds.
5. **The certificate rules are restated, not referenced.** The policy must be complete on its own; the coa rules 4 to 7 appear in it as the certificate rules, with the same wording.
6. **Workbook semantics.** The cell's format decides a number's value, because that is what QA sees in the application; hidden rows and sheets stay part of the record, because a filter hides and does not delete. Formula cells carry their stored result and the generator never writes a formula whose stored result disagrees with its inputs, so no rule needs a formula evaluator.

## Calls made after the builder check

A fresh reviewer read the first draft (the single-PDF binder) as the builder. Its six questions and what I decided, carried into this draft where they still apply:

1. **Member routes.** Certificates share the package route; only a `member_*` feature moves one to its own route. The planner keeps one route per item, the reading share means what it says, and a reading miss on a member is explained by a feature.
2. **Time.** The per-item limit becomes a pack parameter (`seconds` in pack.toml, read by test.sh, the reference gate and the Docker reference stage): 300 s for this pack, 120 s unchanged for coa and size-set. The reference target is under 150 s on the slowest scanned package, which the coa reader's depth allows for 12 pages when each page is OCR'd once and reused. Costs if wrong: a slower verifier run, never a void task.
3. **Samples against features.** Features merged where one reader path decides the variants (`qty_unit`, `qty_tolerance`, `coa_conversion`, `label_amount`, `allergen_wrong`, `lot_mismatch`, `fp_missing`, `coa_expired`, `member_scan`), and `samples_max` set to 18 for 46 features.
4. **Task size.** Scans at 200 dpi for this pack (a docforge parameter per pack; coa keeps 300 so its byte proof holds), hidden 30, PDF pages capped at 12: about 50 MB. The build checks the Terminal-Bench guidance on task size before assembling; the levers if a ceiling bites are the page range and the JPEG quality.
5. **Several findings on one lot.** Every check runs on every lot and `findings` lists every code; the row's `certificate` status takes the first of `missing`, `unapproved`, `expired`, `fail`, `incomplete`. Rules 5 and 12 say so.
6. **Finished-product tests.** A second catalogue in the certificate library, passed to the constructor; coa passes its own, so seed 4001 renders unchanged.

The reviewer's own defaults, adopted as written: the rule 6 mutant keys on the packing date, which the packing record gives; a lot's quantity is rounded before the sum (rule 4); labels print full dates; kestrel's unit habit follows the formula's unit unless `qty_unit` says otherwise, so conversion stays feature-decided; `valid()` holds the page cap. The binder merge from the first draft is dropped.

## Calls made after the second builder check

A second reviewer read the mixed-format draft with the new media in view. Its eight questions and what I decided:

1. **No formulas.** openpyxl writes a formula without a cached result, so a reader opening with values only would see nothing. Every cell is a stored value; the totals row is a plain number and an aid. Rule 2 says so.
2. **Where VOID lives.** A sheet is void by its title or its first cell; a row by any cell reading VOID or SUPERSEDED; a certificate by a VOID stamp on any page, as a whole. CSVs carry no void mark. The feature `void_document` exercises all three forms. Rule 7 names them, so the reader detects exactly that and nothing is a font-style trap.
3. **A forwarded certificate's date** is the forwarded message's own Date header, the one the supplier sent. Supersession is only ever between two emailed certificates, stated in rule 7 as a scope fact, so a bare issue date never competes with an instant and no tie is generated.
4. **Dates as text.** Workbook dates are stored dates; everywhere else a date is ISO or carries an English month name, as in coa; numeric day-month-year never appears. Rule 2 says so.
5. **The join.** A dispensing row gives the formula code, the master's material name, the supplier's full name and the lot. A certificate counts for a row when its issuer, its material and one of its lots match the row's. The row's supplier is what is compared with `approved_suppliers` and what the output copies. Rules 4 and 5 say so.
6. **Label names and the revision table.** The master gains `label_name` per ingredient and the panel row maps by it. The revision table has the amendment block's shape: revision, field, value.
7. **The CSV's shape.** One line per pallet with cases, units per case and loose, a total line as an aid, and a decimal gross-weight column that nothing grades, so `csv_semicolon` decides reading (thousands separators in the integers) and `total_lies` can land on the CSV. Rule 10 says so.
8. **One lot on several rows.** Rows of the same lot are one lot: each row rounded, then summed; order by first row, sheets in tab order. Rule 4 says so.

The reviewer's own defaults, adopted: display rounding at the format is half up; file extensions are honest while base names carry no meaning; the finished-product report is told from a supplier certificate by its product name and its lab's title; the findings sort is a plain string sort; master amounts are strings so their places survive JSON; the task's verifier timeout is `hidden × seconds` plus a margin, written at assembly from the pack.

## Builder changes

- **Items as folders.** `ext = "pkg"` makes an item a directory `ID.pkg/` rather than a file: the renderer writes into it, the assembler and the Docker reference stage copy trees, test.sh loops over `*.pkg`, and the tool takes the folder path. One helper that copies a file or a tree, used wherever an item is copied; `ext` otherwise unchanged for coa and size-set.
- **Certificate document library.** The coa pack's certificate model (`Cert`, `apply_features`, the catalogue, the houses) and its drawing code become a library under `src/taskfactory/forge/` that takes a house definition and a test catalogue rather than a house key and the module's own list, so the manufacturer's lab can be a fifth house per style and the finished-product report draws from its own catalogue. Proof it changed nothing: re-render coa seed 4001 (build 1k) and compare with the shipped build, as was done for the docforge extraction.
- **Three small writers** in the forge: a workbook writer over openpyxl (cell types, number formats, merged cells, hidden rows and sheets, a totals row), a CSV writer (delimiter, decimal mark, quoting), and an email writer over the standard library (multipart, base64 attachments, Date with time zone, reply and forward structures). Each has a reader-side check in the pack's tests: what the writer wrote reads back as the model says.
- **Reference**: the coa page reader and certificate evaluation carried from the coa reference; a workbook reader (openpyxl, stored values rendered with the formats the generator uses: `0`, `0.0`, `0.00`, `0.000`, `#,##0.00`, `#,##0.000`), a CSV reader with delimiter and decimal detection from the header and the data, an email reader that walks parts and extracts attachments and dates; readers for the three manufacturer sets' sheets, panels and records; the cross-checks. Time budget: at most 12 PDF pages per package, slowest package under 150 s on a scan route at 2 CPUs.
- **compare.py**: numbers as exact decimals, `findings` as a list compared in order after sorting both sides, lot normalisation with hyphens dropped.
- **Per-pack limit and scan resolution.** `seconds` and `scan_dpi` in `[pack]`, defaults 120 and 300, read by test.sh and task.toml's verifier timeout (both written into the task at assembly), the reference gate and docforge.
- Nothing else: plan, gates, fix loop, holdout and trials are reused as they are. The task template (Dockerfiles, test.sh, test_outputs.py, instruction template) is the coa one with the item loop and the words changed.

## Open risks

- **Workbook value semantics** must be nailed to a short list of number formats the generator uses, each with a worked sample, or the fairness gate will flag the first cell an agent reads from the stored number. Rule 2 names the semantics; the samples show every format.
- **Agents that ignore the emails.** Any agent that reads only the PDFs and the workbook loses every lot whose certificate came by mail; that is a rule 1 miss, stated and sampled, and the pilot log will show how often it happens.
- **Time.** Certificate scans still cost about 4 s a page in OCR; the 12-page cap and a reference that OCRs a page once keep the slowest package under the 300 s limit with the gate's headroom.
- **Reads as laborious rather than hard.** The weakness map's review risk for this shape. The counter is in the policy: twelve short rules, each with its finding code, and a worked sample for every feature, so the task is long because the package is, never because a rule is hidden.
- **The certificate library extraction** touches a pack that met the bar twice. The byte comparison on seed 4001 is the only acceptable proof; a pixel-level drift from scan noise is fine, a text or layout change is not.
- **Features that pile on one lot.** A package with `eml_reply_supersedes`, `coa_fail` and `member_scan` on the same certificate is fair by the rules but reads as spite; the planner's feature count per item is the control, and the pilot log will say where the agents actually fall.
